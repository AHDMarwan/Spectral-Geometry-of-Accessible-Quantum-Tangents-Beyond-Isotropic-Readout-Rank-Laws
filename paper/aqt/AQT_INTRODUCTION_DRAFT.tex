\section{Introduction}

Variational quantum circuits interact with a classical learning or control loop through measured data. Quantum Fisher information (QFI) and related state-space metrics describe local changes of the quantum state before an experimental measurement is specified \cite{BraunsteinCaves1994,Stokes2020,Haug2021}. A fixed measurement restricts this geometry to the information carried by its outcome distribution, and a subsequent choice of observables or classical features can discard additional tangent directions \cite{BraunsteinCaves1994,HottaOzawa2004,LuLuoOh2012}. This second restriction is the subject of the present work.

Readout restriction is distinct from established mechanisms that alter variational-circuit trainability. Observable locality, circuit controllability, dynamical Lie-algebra structure, and symmetry restrictions can change gradient concentration or the accessible state manifold \cite{Cerezo2021,UvarovBiamonte2021,Larocca2022,HolmesSharma2022}. Randomized measurements can also recover quantum Fisher geometry after averaging over measurement bases \cite{LuSha2025}. Here the quantum measurement is held fixed. The question is what remains visible after its outcome record is compressed to a readout subspace of fixed dimension.
