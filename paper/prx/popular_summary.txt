Popular Summary

Parameterized quantum circuits can be very sensitive to changes in their parameters even when a chosen measurement reveals only a small part of that sensitivity. This distinction matters for quantum machine learning, where a model is trained from measured observables rather than from direct access to the quantum state.

We study the covariance of measurement scores produced by quantum-state tangents and ask how much of it survives a restricted readout. For a readout of fixed rank, random relative orientation gives a simple reference value. We show mathematically that concentration around this reference does not require an isotropic tangent covariance. Numerical tests on several circuit families agree with the rank reference over a broad finite-size range even as their tangent spectra become strongly anisotropic. The same tests also identify architecture-dependent departures: an RY--RZ--CZ circuit shows a reproducible one-body deficit, while a half-filled U(1)-conserving circuit retains much more information in low-weight diagonal observables. A separate spectral calculation confirms that, for generic circuits, more tangent information is available at the same rank than the physical low-weight readout captures.

The results provide a way to distinguish limited information in the quantum tangent space from information that exists but is poorly aligned with the measured observables. They do not establish a barren plateau for a supervised loss; that would require a separate scaling study of loss-gradient variance. The framework can instead be used to compare circuit architectures and readout choices before or alongside a training experiment.
